Kidney Stones & Endourology

Kidney Stone Treatment Questions

Very small stones (< 5 mm) often pass with hydration and medical therapy. Stones up to 1 cm may be treated with non-invasive ESWL or scar-free RIRS laser surgery, ureteric stones with URSL, and larger 2–5 cm stones with Mini-Perc or standard keyhole PCNL. A scan (ultrasound/CT KUB) guides the safest choice.
Yes. RIRS (Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery) passes a slender flexible ureteroscope through the natural urinary passage into the kidney without making any external skin incisions, using a laser to dust the stone finely.
Standard PCNL uses a keyhole tract under 1 cm for large renal stones (2–5 cm), while Mini-Perc / Ultra-Mini Perc uses a thinner 5 mm tract and miniature nephroscope to reduce discomfort and accelerate recovery.
Prostate & Pelvic Health

Prostate & Urogynaecology Questions

If you notice a weak stream, straining to start urination, frequent nighttime waking, urgency, or a sensation of incomplete bladder emptying, an evaluation with uroflowmetry, ultrasound, and PSA check helps establish whether medication or minimally invasive procedures like HoLEP, TURP, or UroLift are needed.
Yes. Nearly 85–90% of urogynaecological conditions respond well to targeted lifestyle care, pelvic floor strengthening, medications, or brief minimally invasive sling support procedures like TOT or TVT.
