Understanding the Prostate

Why Prostate Enlargement Affects Urination

The prostate gland sits just below the bladder and surrounds the upper part of the urethra. As men age, benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) often causes the gland to enlarge and press against the urethra—leading to a weak stream, frequent nighttime urination, urgency, or incomplete bladder emptying.

When medication alone no longer gives adequate relief, modern transurethral procedures can restore smooth flow with minimal recovery downtime.

Treatment Pathways

4 Proven Surgical & Minimally Invasive Options

Option 01 · Gold Standard

1. Transurethral Resection of the Prostate (TURP)

Performed under spinal or general anesthesia with zero external incisions. A slender resectoscope passes through the urethra to trim away obstructing prostate tissue while gently cauterizing blood vessels. A catheter typically stays in place for 1–2 days, and most patients return home shortly thereafter with markedly improved urinary flow.

Option 02 · Ideal for Large Prostates

2. Holmium Laser Enucleation of the Prostate (HoLEP)

HoLEP uses a precision holmium laser through a resectoscope to cleanly separate entire overgrown prostate lobes from the capsule, which are then removed via a morcellator. It minimizes bleeding, achieves thorough tissue clearance—even in very large prostates—and provides durable long-term relief.

Option 03 · Low Bleeding Risk

3. Transurethral Laser Resection or Vaporisation

Using GreenLight or Thulium laser fibers guided through a cystoscope, focused light energy vaporizes obstructing prostate tissue cleanly. Because bleeding risk is remarkably low, this approach is particularly well-suited for patients taking blood-thinning medications, often enabling same-day or next-day discharge.

Option 04 · Tissue-Preserving

4. Prostatic Urethral Lift (PUL / UroLift) Implants

A minimally invasive alternative that neither cuts nor heats prostate tissue. Tiny permanent implants gently lift and hold enlarged lobes away from the urethra, widening the urinary channel while keeping sexual function and ejaculation intact—with return to normal activities within days.
