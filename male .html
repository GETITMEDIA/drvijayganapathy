Why Male Assessment Matters

Root-Cause Evaluation Before Costly Treatments

Up to 15% of couples experience difficulty conceiving, and male factors contribute to one-third to one-half of all cases. Common causes include low sperm production, abnormal sperm function, varicocele, hormonal imbalances, or blockages preventing sperm delivery.

Identifying the underlying cause before jumping into costly interventions is essential so treatment can target what truly matters.

When to Consult

Common Symptoms & Signs of Male Infertility

  • Difficulty with ejaculation, low libido, or erectile dysfunction
  • Pain, swelling, or lump in the testicle area
  • Low sperm count (under 15 million sperm/mL)
  • Decreased body or facial hair / hormonal indicators
  • Abnormal breast growth (gynecomastia)
  • Recurrent respiratory infections or inability to smell
  • Inability to conceive after 12 months of trying
Specialized Interventions

Treatments for Male Infertility

1. Sperm Retrieval (PESA, MESA, TESA, TESE)

When sperm is absent in the ejaculate, minimally invasive microsurgical or needle retrieval collects viable sperm directly from the epididymis or testis.

2. Microsurgery & Varicocelectomy

Surgical repair of enlarged scrotal veins (varicocele) or microsurgical blockage correction (vasovasostomy / vasoepididymostomy) to restore natural sperm passage.

3. Treating Infections

Targeted antibiotic therapy clears bacterial infections of the reproductive tract that impair sperm motility or cause ductal inflammation.

4. Sexual Function Care

Evidence-based medical therapy and counseling to resolve erectile or ejaculatory difficulties affecting natural conception.

5. Hormonal Therapy

Hormone replacement or endocrine modulation when pituitary or testicular hormone imbalances impair sperm production.

6. Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)

Coordinated preparation and surgical sperm retrieval supporting IUI, IVF, or ICSI cycles with fertility centers.
