Male Infertility

Infertility in males is a common cause of childlessness. Male factors contribute to one-third to one-half of all infertility cases in couples. An accurate assessment of the male partner is crucial for proper diagnosis and treatment.

Up to 15% of couples are infertile, and in over a third of these cases, male infertility plays a role. Causes include low sperm production, abnormal sperm function, or blockages that prevent sperm delivery. Health issues, lifestyle, or medical conditions may also contribute.

The main sign of male infertility is the inability to conceive a child. However, some cases may include other symptoms caused by underlying medical conditions.

Common Symptoms of Male Infertility

  • Problems with sexual function (e.g. difficulty with ejaculation, low libido, erectile dysfunction)
  • Pain, swelling, or lump in the testicle area
  • Recurrent respiratory infections
  • Inability to smell
  • Abnormal breast growth (gynecomastia)
  • Decreased body or facial hair
  • Low sperm count (under 15 million sperm/mL)

Diagnosis is essential before starting any costly treatment. Identifying the specific cause helps guide proper therapy.

Treatments for Male Infertility

1. Sperm Retrieval Procedures (PESA, MESA, TESA, TESE)

These are used when sperm is not present in the semen. Sperm can be retrieved directly from the testicle or epididymis through minimally invasive procedures.

2. Surgery

  • Varicocelectomy: Repair of varicocele (enlarged veins)
  • Vasovasostomy / Vasoepididymostomy: Reversal of vasectomy or blockage correction

3. Treating Infections

Antibiotic therapy may treat infections in the reproductive tract, though fertility may not always be restored.

4. Sexual Function Treatments

Medications or counseling can improve fertility issues caused by erectile dysfunction or premature ejaculation.

5. Hormonal Therapy

Hormone replacement or medication is used when hormone imbalances cause infertility.

6. Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)

ART involves retrieving sperm through ejaculation or surgery and using it for intrauterine insemination (IUI), in vitro fertilization (IVF), or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).
